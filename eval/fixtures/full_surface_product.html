SENTINEL-PRODUCT-TEXT. Echo One is the first speaker we have shipped with a calibration microphone that stays in the cabinet. It listens to the room for eleven seconds, writes a correction curve to onboard memory and plays it back without asking your phone to do anything.

The correction is conservative on purpose. We cap the low-frequency lift at 4 dB and leave the presence region alone, because a curve that fixes a measured problem should not invent a new one. Owners in treated rooms can turn the whole system off from the Northwind app and get the raw response.

The 6.5 inch long-throw woofer runs from a 120 W class-D amplifier and a ported enclosure tuned to 38 Hz. At reference level it reaches 118 dB SPL at one metre, which is louder than most living rooms tolerate and far louder than the previous model managed before the limiter stepped in.

A hardware mute switch disconnects the microphone capsule at the board, not in software. The LED beside it is wired to the same trace, so an unlit ring means the capsule has no path to the processor. We publish the schematic for the audio board on the support site.

Streaming runs over Wi-Fi 6 in the 5 GHz band, with AirPlay 2, Chromecast and Spotify Connect handled natively. Bluetooth 5.3 is available as a fallback for guests, though it disables multi-room sync because the codecs do not share a common clock.

Multi-room groups hold sync within 8 ms across up to eight units, measured with a measurement microphone at the listening position. Groups survive a router reboot: each speaker caches its group membership in flash and rejoins when the access point comes back.

The fabric wrap is a recycled PET weave that can be peeled off and washed. Underneath, six Torx screws hold the baffle; the power inlet, the amplifier board and the Wi-Fi module are each separate boards joined by ribbon cables, so a failed component does not condemn the cabinet.

Setup takes about three minutes if your phone is already on the 2.4 GHz provisioning network. The app scans a QR code printed inside the base, hands over credentials and then moves the speaker to your main network. There is no account required for local playback.

Voice control works with the assistant of your choice. Northwind ships an open intent bridge that maps wake words to whichever service you enable, and the bridge runs locally so audio never leaves the house unless you explicitly turn on remote processing.

For listeners who care about numbers, we measure every production unit on the line and print the serial-numbered result on the box insert. The scattered plot for the first two thousand units sits in the lab notes below.

Shipping is free in the contiguous United States and takes two business days once the order leaves our Reno warehouse. Returns are accepted for sixty days with no restocking fee, and we pay the return label if the speaker arrived damaged.

The five-year warranty covers parts and labour, including the power supply and the Wi-Fi module. Out-of-warranty repairs are flat-rate: $59 for an amplifier board, $39 for a power board, and free if the failure is a manufacturing defect we can see under the microscope.

Set it up in three minutes

Pairing is the part most owners get wrong, usually because the phone stays on 5 GHz while the speaker listens on 2.4 GHz for the first thirty seconds. The app warns you, but the speaker also flashes amber twice to say it heard the beacon.

Questions owners ask

Warranty and returns

Can I use Echo One without the app?
Yes. The speaker boots into its last Wi-Fi network and plays from any AirPlay, Chromecast or Spotify Connect source. The app is only required for calibration, groups and firmware.
Does calibration run every time I move it?
Only when you tell it to. A stored curve keeps playing until you tap Recalibrate in the app or hold the top button for three seconds.
What happens if the power board fails in year four?
We ship the board and a guide, or you can mail the speaker to Reno for a flat $39 repair. The cabinet is designed so the board slides out without touching the woofer.
Is the speaker loud enough for a party?
In a 40 square metre room it holds 96 dB at the far wall with headroom left. Past that the limiter engages and distortion rises quickly.
Why is there no battery?
A battery large enough for four hours would add 700 grams and shorten the woofer's throw. We chose a replaceable cord instead.
Can two Echo Ones make a stereo pair?
Yes, from firmware 1.6. Pairing takes eleven seconds and the app keeps the two units phase-aligned automatically.
Do you ship to PO boxes?
Not for speakers, because the carrier will not take a 3.4 kg parcel to a PO box. Headphones and parts ship to PO boxes.
How long are firmware updates supported?
Seven years from the last sale date of the model, with security patches for the Wi-Fi stack beyond that.

A short buying guide

Choosing between the three wireless speakers comes down to room size and how much of the bass you want to feel. The table at the end of this section lists the numbers, but the listening test matters more, and we run one every Saturday at the Reno store with the lights off and the calibration curve engaged.

Echo One suits rooms between 18 and 40 square metres. Below 18 square metres the woofer loads the near wall too hard and the correction has to pull more than 4 dB out of the 60 to 90 Hz region, which costs a little clarity. Above 40 square metres you start wanting a second unit or the Sub One.

Beam is the same electronics in a smaller box with a 4 inch woofer and no calibration microphone. It keeps the repairable construction and the app, but it cannot fix a room on its own. If your listening position is within two metres of the speaker, Beam is the better buy and the difference in bass is smaller than the price gap suggests.

Aster is the floorstanding three-way, 104 cm tall, with a 10 inch woofer and a separate midrange driver. It is the only one of the three that fills a 60 square metre open plan room without the limiter. It costs $170 more than Echo One and weighs 11 kg, so measure your doorway before ordering.

All three share the same streaming stack, the same firmware train and the same seven-year support promise. A mixed group works: put an Echo One in the living room and a Beam in the kitchen and they hold sync within 8 ms, because the sync master can be either model.

If you already own passive speakers, Relay Amp runs the same room engine through a two-channel amplifier and a measurement microphone on a tripod. It is the choice for listeners who have already committed to a cabinet and do not want to replace it.

When Beam is the better choice

Beam earns its place in bedrooms, studies and apartments where the walls are close and the volume rarely passes 80 dB. Its smaller port does not chuff at low levels, and the absence of a calibration microphone means nothing to switch off in a room that does not need correcting.

Owners who bought Beam after returning Echo One tell us the same thing: the bass they missed on paper they did not miss in the room. The crossover sits at 2.8 kHz instead of 2.1 kHz, so the dome tweeter works slightly harder, but at conversational levels the difference is a half-decibel of presence.

Beam also ships in a white wrap that matches the Echo One white, so a mixed install does not look mismatched on a shelf. The white fabric stains more easily and costs $10 more to replace.

When you need the Sub One

Sub One takes over below 63 Hz, which is the frequency where room modes do the most damage. Adding it lets Echo One's own woofer stop trying to pressurise the room, and the result is usually cleaner midbass even at the same measured output.

Wire the Sub One over Ethernet if you can. The wireless link adds 6 ms and, in houses with crowded 5 GHz spectrum, occasional dropouts that sound like a tiny gap in a kick drum. The app shows link quality and will warn you before it becomes audible.

Two Sub Ones in opposite corners flatten the axial mode at 47 Hz that almost every 28 square metre room has. We tested the pair in the chamber with pink noise and a ground-plane microphone; the 40 to 80 Hz ripple dropped from 7.2 dB to 2.1 dB.

Sub One does not include a calibration microphone of its own. Echo One measures the room with the sub switched on and writes one combined curve to both units, which is why the pairing step asks you to leave the sub powered during the eleven second sweep.

Troubleshooting

The six problems that account for most support calls, with the fix we would try first. If none of these help, the support team answers within one business day.

Common setup problems

The speaker flashes amber and never connects
The phone stayed on 5 GHz. Enable the 2.4 GHz band on your router, hold mute for eight seconds to reset provisioning, and start the pairing again. The amber double flash means the beacon was heard but the band is wrong.
Calibration sounds worse than before
The microphone was probably covered. Remove anything sitting on the top grille, run the sweep again, and check that the app reports eleven seconds of capture. A three second capture means the capsule did not hear the sweep.
Multi-room audio drifts apart
One unit is on 2.4 GHz and the others on 5 GHz. The app shows per-unit link quality; move the straggler to the 5 GHz network or wire it with Ethernet and the group re-syncs in about ten seconds.
Bluetooth skips when the phone is in a pocket
Bluetooth antenna sits behind the rear port tube. Keep the phone on the same side as the front baffle, or switch to Wi-Fi streaming, which is the designed path for anything longer than a phone call.
The line out buzzes with the amplifier on
Ground loop. Unplug the USB-C service cable, which is the usual return path, or insert a ground isolator on the 3.5 mm run. The line out is fixed level, so the buzz is not a volume problem.
Firmware update stalls at 60 percent
Power cycle at the wall, not the speaker. The update resumes from the last verified block. If it stalls twice more, hold mute for eight seconds and the speaker falls back to the previous firmware and reports the failure to support.
